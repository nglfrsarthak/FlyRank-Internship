0. Abstract

Question: which visible Google pages will lose >20% of impressions next half-month? Data: FlyRank pseudonymized warehouse (78M daily facts, March 2026 slice, decision moment Mar 15). Method: 7-feature Random Forest vs a hand-written baseline on the same stratified split, leakage-checked tight. Result: model lifts discrimination over the baseline (ROC AUC + precision@K reported from your run; base rate ~0.29 shown beside every number). Output: a ranked CSV playbook an editor can work top-down tomorrow.

1. Introduction / Problem statement

Editors don't need another dashboard — they need to know what to review first. The decision this work supports is triage: at the close of Mar 15, order every page that was visible in the first half by measured risk of a >20% impression drop in the second half. Unit is one content item. A wrong call wastes editor time, not traffic — so precision@K matters more than accuracy.

3. Methodology

Features (7, all knowable at decision moment): imp_first15, clk_first15, avg_pos_first15, pos_vol_first15, days_seen_first15, ctr_first15, content_age_days.

Label proxy: is_declining_proxy = (imp_last16 < 0.8 × imp_first15) — mirrors the starter slice's >20% rule for trend_direction == "down". Baseline: score = visible × (stale + slipping) with stale = content_age_days ≥ 180, slipping = 10 < avg_pos ≤ 20, reason stale_visible_slipping, action refresh_candidate. Validation: stratified train_test_split (test 25%, seed 42) on the same March slice as the baseline; model = RandomForest(n_estimators=200). Leakage checks: no query-table columns, no future dates, no label-derived inputs, IDs only for grouping.

5. Limitations & honest framing

Directional, not causal. March survivors with ≥100 early impressions only — unbalanced panel under-represents young clients. Half-month split can read seasonality as momentum. Low-volume CTR/position remains noisy even with floors. The warehouse is pseudonymized and frozen at 2026-07-03; freshness lag of 3 days is baked in.
